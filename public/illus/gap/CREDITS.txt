# Gap story store photography: credits, choices, cut

Photo edit for the invented linen store in `GapStory` (homepage hero, product grid, welcome email).
Every final is from Pexels under the Pexels licence. Nothing in any final shows a person, a logo or readable text.

## 0. The brand name: Fernleaf collides, rename it

"Fernleaf" is a real brand name already in use:

- **Wayfair UK sells home goods under its own "Fernleaf" brand**: throws, cushions, duvet covers, rugs, furniture, priced in £. That is our exact category in our exact market.
  https://www.wayfair.co.uk/brand/bnd/fernleaf-b59565.html
- **Fonterra's Fernleaf** is a major dairy brand in Southeast Asia.
  https://www.fonterra.com/sea/en/our-stories/media/Fernleaf-Proteinmilk-powder.html

**Suggested replacement: Hollin, at `hollin.example`.** Hollin is the Northern English dialect word for holly. A search found no apparel, homeware or linen brand by that name. The only notable use is a small UK behavioural consultancy (hollin.co.uk) in an unrelated field.

Two other names were checked and rejected:
- **Teasel**: "Teasel Home" already sells blankets and cushion covers.
- **Heddle**: too close to Heddels, a menswear site.

The final name belongs to whoever owns the brand slot. `fernleaf.example` currently appears in `src/content/pages/home.ts` and `marketing.ts`.

## 1. Briefs (written before searching)

| Slot | What the photograph has to do that the copy cannot |
|---|---|
| (a) Homepage hero, 16:9 desktop and 4:5 phone | In one glance the store has to read as a real brand with taste: linen lived in, morning light, warm. The other tools only see this window as a frozen snapshot. The image must still read at about 300px wide inside the dark scene, so it needs one subject and big tonal shapes. |
| (b) Product grid, 4:5, three or four tiles | The range as a buyer meets it: a shirt, the bedding, an object for the home, all in one light, one palette and one grade. It should look like a catalogue a merchandiser signed off, not four separate searches. Each object needs a silhouette that reads at about 130px. |
| (c) Welcome email header, 2:1 | A close, tactile detail from the hero's world, so the email in the agent's inbox reads as the same brand's own send. It needs to be warm enough that the silence after it lands. |

## 3. The cut, and why

- **Posed or eye contact.** Most of the people shots went for this. Burst's white-shirt-by-the-water series smiles to the lens (24978, 24979, 24986). So do px 29963935, px 7445142 (sleep-mask smile) and Burst woman-waking-up.
- **Dated.** px 821649 (2018 flat lay). Burst bedroom-in-neutral-tones has a 2015-style room with a ceiling fan. Burst headphones-and-laptop-on-bed and px 4050387 show a laptop.
- **Another brand, or readable text.** These were cut:
  - px 4050387: a VOGUE print on the wall.
  - px 5637630: a "Restaurant" awning.
  - px 37704838–51: a white tee with a visible patch label.
  - px 1112378: a slogan shirt.
  - px 32795039–61: a children's brand's product shoot.
  - px 27399621: a throw-blanket seller's ad.
- **Wrong story.** px 9462626 shows hotel housekeeping. px 28576616–36 is a cleaning and ironing series. Neither belongs on a linen brand's homepage.
- **Incoherent grade or light.**
  - Burst's house style is cool, high-key white, and its backdrops are loud: orange tee 17873, pink wall 10754, peach mug.
  - px 4045542 and px 33312399 are beautiful, but too cold, or too orange with a dark wood headboard.
  - px 7445114–45 (Katrin Bolovtsova) is coherent, but white and cool, and it reads stock.
- **Unreadable at 130px.** Texture-only frames went: px 7794365, 8774406, 6630897, 19743612, 8793483. So did the e-reader frames from the chosen shoot (31145665–70), which also show a device.
- **Near misses kept on record:**
  - Burst `unmade-dusty-pink-bedding` (19548) is the closest Burst frame and a good alternate for the bedding tile.
  - Burst `woman-resting-her-feet-by-the-window` (10286) is a lovely candid frame, but it is knitwear in green light, not this world.
  - px 15764810–13 (cream blouse holding folded linen) is warm, but blouse-specific.
- **Two shoots win, so the grid is coherent:**
  - **Letícia Alvares, 14 Mar 2025.** A blush-plaster bedroom with an iron bed, a hard morning sun slash, a grey stoneware mug and an oak board. One session gives the hero, the phone hero, two product tiles and the email.
  - **dayong tien.** Linen shirts on a wooden hanger hung from a branch, against a warm plaster wall. Collars were checked at full resolution and carry no labels. The photographer is an individual (also on Unsplash as tian dayong), not a brand.
  - The two walls already share a hue, 18–24°. The grade closes the remaining gap in exposure. The grid reads the way real brands shoot: apparel in the studio, homeware in the room.

**No people in any final.** The agent is the only visitor in this story. An empty, sunlit, slept-in bed says "lived in" without making the hero about a model. It also avoids any identifiable person, so the casting question does not arise.

## 5. Grade (`work/grade.py`, applied at full resolution before resizing)

1. **Pre-balance per shoot.**
   - **Studio shirts:** RGB gain ×(1.00, 0.978, 0.945) and gamma 1.10. This warms and slightly darkens the studio wall towards the bedroom's blush.
   - **Bedroom:** shadow lift of 0.05 and gamma 0.94, so the tiles read inside the dark Obsession scene.
   - **Email still life:** shot cooler than the rest, so it gets a warm gain of ×(1.02, 1.00, 0.962).
2. **Shared family look.**
   - A matte tone curve: black point 9, white point 247, and a gentle S of 0.08.
   - A light split tone: about +3R/−2.5B in the shadows and +2R/−2B in the highlights.
   - Saturation ×0.93.
   - After resizing, a light sharpen (unsharp mask, radius 0.7, 55%).

The grade is meant to go unnoticed: `grade_before_after.jpg` shows how small the change is. It is not a filter.

Rebuild everything with `cd work && python3 build.py`. Crops live in `work/crops.py`.
