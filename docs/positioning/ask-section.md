# The ask section: copy, and the review behind it

Written 22 Oct 2026. It replaces "Your AI agent can think. It can't be a customer for thirty days." on Home, and adds the same section to Marketing, Sales and Agencies. On every page it sits directly above "Other tools read the outside. Obsession is the customer." where that section exists.

## What James asked for

- **Sell the data.** The old section sold the plumbing: tabs for MCP, a workflow file and code, plus five verb names. The reader came away knowing how to connect, not what they'd get. A question like "Compare our Black Friday emails with our five closest rivals" only has an answer if someone kept those emails. That's the point to sell.
- **Think of it like Exa or Lessie.** Exa is "the world's data for AI agents". Lessie lets you "describe who you're looking for in plain English" and get people back. Obsession is the same idea for what companies send and do to their customers.
- **Put it where the buyers are.** Marketing, Sales and Agencies need this most, so it goes on those pages and on Home.
- **Don't say "your AI agent".** It doesn't tell a reader that it means Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT, Grok or Muse.

## The term for the AI

Options weighed:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "your AI agent" | Out. Buyers don't say it (COPY.md lists "AI agent" as a word buyers never use), and it doesn't call to mind the tool they actually have open. |
| "your assistant" | Too vague, and it sounds like a person. |
| "your AI tools" | Reads like a software category, not the window they type in. |
| **"whichever AI you already use", after naming two** | Chosen. The names do the recognising: Claude Code and Codex, the tools people work in (James, 22 Oct: "Claude" and "ChatGPT" read as the models). The phrase covers the rest: Cursor, Grok, Muse and whatever ships next. The section's "Ask from" row then lists them all. |

## The review

Each draft was checked against these questions, and changed where it failed.

1. **Does the headline sell the data, not the plumbing?** The first draft led with the AI ("Claude can search the web. Your rivals' emails were never on it."), which put the AI back in the spotlight. The next, "Ask what any company sends its customers", typed its middle phrase in turn and was dropped (22 Oct). The heading is now about the data itself: "The commercial intelligence only a customer gets." The label above it names the areas, and the line says how to ask and that every answer comes with its receipts.
2. **Does it say why an AI on its own can't answer?** Yes: "none of it is on the web, so no AI can search for it". That's the old headline's real point ("it can't be a customer for thirty days"), said in the buyer's terms.
3. **Is it technical?** No code, no verb names and no MCP command in the section itself. The four ways in and the five verbs stay on /developers, which keeps the old section. A single small link sends builders there.
4. **Is every claim true as designed?**
   - "Any company" is honest because of the not-yet line: an agent signs up the day you ask.
   - There are no coverage numbers, because there's no measured figure yet.
   - Every company in the examples is invented (Fern Hollow, Wrenfield, Northfold, Larchwise), as on the rest of the site.
   - Stack changes, support reply times, the Gmail tab and checkout offers are all in system.md.
5. **Does each page speak to its reader?**
   - Marketing asks about competitors' emails, prices and welcome series.
   - Sales asks for an account brief, support speed across a target list, and stack changes.
   - Agencies asks about pitch evidence, Promotions placement across clients, and a broken reminder.
   - Home mixes one question from each.
6. **Voice:**
   - British spelling;
   - no dashes and no hyphenated compounds ("sign up", "pay later", "win back");
   - numerals in data;
   - "receipts" always sits beside the plain words ("14 emails and 3 pages");
   - none of the banned words.
7. **Does "Asked in" show the doors without explaining them?** Each example names a different place (Claude Code, Codex, code). The two chat windows also show the one line that connected Obsession to that tool (`claude mcp add obsession …`, `codex mcp add obsession --url …`), so a reader sees it takes one step (James, 22 Oct). That says "use it where you already work" with no setup step.
